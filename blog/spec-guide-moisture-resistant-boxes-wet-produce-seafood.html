Spec Guide: Moisture-Resistant Boxes for Wet Produce and Seafood

Waxproduceboxes Editorial • October 2026

Packaging wet produce and seafood is one of the most demanding applications in the cold chain. The box must resist constant moisture, survive icing and hydrocooling, hold its load rating in deep stacks, and drain cleanly between uses. This spec reference walks procurement and quality teams through the dimensions that actually determine performance, so you can write a specification that holds up in the field rather than just on paper.

Wall Thickness and Gauge

For reusable PP boxes, wall construction is the single biggest driver of durability and load rating. Twin-wall corrugated PP is typically specified by its board weight in grams per square meter, while solid-wall tubs are specified by nominal wall thickness in millimeters. Heavier board and thicker walls resist crushing and puncture but add weight and cube. For iced seafood and dense wet produce, favor the upper end of the range to maintain compression strength when the box is saturated and cold. The moisture-resistant produce packaging alternatives from Elipacko are available in multiple board and wall specifications so you can match construction to the weight and handling profile of the commodity.

Load Ratings and Stacking Strength

Specify load ratings in two ways: dynamic load for handling and transport, and static compression for warehouse stacking. The critical number is retained compression strength at cold-chain temperature and high humidity, because that is the real operating condition. Unlike wax corrugated, which loses strength as it absorbs ambient moisture, PP holds its rated strength because the resin does not take on water. Require your supplier to state compression performance at refrigerated conditions, not just at ambient, and verify it against your worst-case pallet height.

Drainage and Ventilation

Wet applications demand engineered drainage. Specify drain holes or an open-weave base so meltwater and rinse water exit rather than pooling against the product. For iced goods, a ventilated sidewall pattern promotes even cooling and prevents warm pockets. These features also speed washing and drying between trips, which keeps the box in a food-safe condition. When you draft the spec, call out hole placement and open area percentage explicitly rather than leaving ventilation to the molder's discretion.

Cold-Chain Temperature Performance

Polypropylene remains tough and dimensionally stable across the refrigerated and near-freezing range typical of wet produce and seafood. For deep-frozen applications, confirm the resin grade is rated for low-temperature impact resistance, since some PP formulations become brittle in extreme cold. Operations running long or multi-leg chains should coordinate box selection with their broader cold chain and moisture-resistant packaging strategy so pallet patterns, insulation, and box strength all align to the temperature profile.

Food-Contact Compliance

Any box touching unwrapped produce or seafood must use food-grade, FDA-compliant resin. Specify this explicitly and request documentation. For reusable formats, also confirm the resin tolerates repeated hot-water and sanitizer washing without leaching or degrading, since a box that fails hygiene validation is worthless regardless of its structural rating.

Sizing and Sourcing

Finally, specify dimensions that match your pallet footprint and case count so the box integrates with existing lines and storage. A direct manufacturer offering custom sizing can build to your exact internal dimensions, eliminating wasted cube and the need to re-engineer handling equipment. Write the specification around these variables, require documented performance at cold-chain conditions, and you will field a moisture-resistant box that outlasts and outperforms coated corrugated.
