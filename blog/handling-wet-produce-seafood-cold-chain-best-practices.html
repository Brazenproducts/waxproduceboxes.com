Handling Wet Produce and Seafood: Cold-Chain Best Practices

Waxproduceboxes Editorial • October 2026

Moisture Is the Enemy, Not the Cold

Operators new to wet-produce and seafood logistics often assume temperature is the only variable that matters. In practice, moisture management is just as critical. Ice melt, product exudate, and condensation from refrigeration cycles all pool inside packaging, and when that packaging is a wax-coated box, the barrier eventually fails at the seams and fiber begins to break down. This how-to walks through the handling practices that protect product quality while extending packaging life, using reusable polypropylene boxes as the working example.

The foundation is choosing non-absorbent packaging from the start. Moisture-proof cold chain and moisture-resistant packaging does not wick, swell, or delaminate, so it handles standing water and repeated icing without structural loss. That property is what makes the following practices reliable.

Step One: Pre-Chill Everything

Bring packaging, product, and staging area to temperature before packing. Warm boxes introduced into a cold load generate condensation that drips onto product and promotes microbial growth. Pre-chilling PP boxes is simple because the material reaches equilibrium quickly and holds no residual moisture from a previous cycle once properly dried.

Step Two: Manage Drainage and Icing

For iced product, specify boxes with drainage features or pair solid boxes with a controlled liner strategy so melt does not accumulate against product. Reusable PP construction tolerates direct contact with ice and seafood liquor that would saturate and collapse a corrugated box within hours.

  • Pre-chill all packaging to load temperature before packing
  • Allow for drainage or control melt with the right liner
  • Avoid overpacking, which restricts cold-air circulation
  • Maintain unbroken temperature from dock to delivery

Step Three: Build Stable, Ventilated Pallets

Cold air must circulate to pull field heat and hold temperature. Align box vents when stacking so airflow channels run uninterrupted through the pallet. Reusable PP boxes hold their dimensions when wet and cold, so vent alignment stays true through the trip, unlike softened corrugate that slumps and blocks airflow.

Step Four: Sanitation Between Cycles

Reusable packaging only pays off if it is cleaned properly between uses, and seafood and wet produce demand rigorous sanitation. PP withstands hot-water washdown, caustic detergents, and sanitizing chemistry that would destroy coated board. A documented wash-and-dry protocol keeps boxes food-safe and ready for the next load. Because the material is non-porous, it does not harbor odor or bacteria the way absorbent packaging can.

Step Five: Close the Loop

The final practice is disciplined empty recovery. A reusable system delivers its ROI only when boxes return for the next cycle. Build retrieval into the lane plan, track box counts, and keep a modest buffer fleet to absorb peak demand. With non-absorbent packaging, proper pre-chilling, aligned ventilation, and a solid sanitation protocol, wet-produce and seafood shippers can hold product quality tighter than wax boxes ever allowed while steadily driving down packaging cost per trip.
