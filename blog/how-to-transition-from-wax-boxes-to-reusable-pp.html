How to Transition Your Operation from Wax Boxes to Reusable PP

Waxproduceboxes Editorial • November 2026

Plan the Transition as a Project, Not a Purchase

Switching from wax-coated produce boxes to reusable polypropylene packaging is not a swap you make on a single purchase order. It is an operational change that touches pack-out, transport, warehousing, and sanitation. Teams that treat it as a structured project capture the savings quickly; teams that just buy crates and hope tend to lose assets and get frustrated. This guide walks through the practical steps to make the transition stick.

Step 1: Pick a Pilot Lane

Do not convert your entire operation at once. Choose one high-volume, closed lane where produce moves from a known origin to a known destination and empties can be returned predictably. Wet produce and seafood lanes are ideal candidates because that is exactly where wax boxes perform worst and degrade fastest, so the contrast is clearest. Run the pilot for one full season to gather real cost-per-trip and loss data before scaling.

Step 2: Right-Size the Box

Wax cartons come in standard footprints, but reusable programs benefit from boxes sized to your pallet pattern and your product. A box that cubes out a pallet cleanly cuts freight cost on every load. As a direct manufacturer, Elipacko supplies produce packaging alternatives from Elipacko in custom sizes, so you can match the internal dimensions to your produce count and the external footprint to your pallet. Lock the dimensions during the pilot so your reverse logistics and storage racks are planned around the final spec.

Step 3: Build the Return Flow

The most common reason reusable programs fail is that nobody designed how empties come back. Before the first full box ships, map the backhaul: which trucks return empty, where boxes consolidate, and who is accountable at each handoff.

  • Backhaul on inbound trucks so return freight is near-zero marginal cost.
  • Nest or stack empties to return several in the space of one full box.
  • Assign ownership at each node to control shrinkage.
  • Consider deposits to incentivize prompt returns.

Step 4: Set Up Wash and Inspection

Because the same box contacts produce repeatedly, every box needs a documented wash-and-inspect step before it re-enters service. Dense food-grade PP cleans fully and dries fast in mechanized wash lines, which supports produce safety audits. For refrigerated and wet applications, boxes built for cold chain and moisture-resistant packaging hold up to repeated washing and refrigeration without absorbing water or harboring bacteria the way a degraded wax carton would.

Step 5: Measure and Scale

At the end of the pilot, compare real numbers: cost-per-trip, shrinkage rate, product damage, and disposal savings. Wax boxes carry hidden costs from landfill fees on waterlogged cartons and from product loss when a wet box fails. Put those against the reusable program's capital cost amortized over actual lifetime trips plus wash and return. For most wet-produce lanes, the reusable system pencils out within one to two seasons, at which point you scale to additional lanes using the playbook you validated.
