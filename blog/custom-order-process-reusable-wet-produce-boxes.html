The Custom Order Process for Reusable Wet-Produce and Seafood Boxes

Waxproduceboxes Editorial • November 2026

Why Custom Beats Off-the-Shelf for Wet Produce

Wax-coated corrugated boxes earned their place in wet-produce and seafood logistics because they shed water better than plain cardboard. But they are single-use, non-recyclable in most waste streams, and expensive over time. Reusable polypropylene boxes solve the moisture and durability problem permanently — and because PP is molded or fabricated to spec, buyers can tailor dimensions, drainage, and venting to their exact commodity. For procurement teams making the switch, understanding the custom order process removes the uncertainty and keeps the transition on schedule.

Step 1: Define the Application Profile

Every custom order begins with a clear application profile. Document the commodity (leafy greens, root vegetables, iced seafood), the typical and maximum load weight, the handling environment, and whether the box will see ice, free water, or wash-down cleaning. Note the pallet footprint and stack height so the box integrates cleanly with your existing material-handling equipment. The more precisely you describe the use case, the tighter the resulting specification. Elipacko's team works from this profile to recommend a format — explore the range of produce packaging alternatives from Elipacko as a starting point.

Step 2: Specify Dimensions and Features

With the profile set, translate it into a specification. Key parameters include internal and external dimensions, wall thickness or flute gauge, load rating, drainage or vent patterns for wet commodities, and stacking lugs or interlocks. For iced seafood, drainage holes and a sump design prevent melt water from pooling; for leafy greens, ventilation balances cooling airflow against dehydration. This is also where you lock in food-grade, FDA-compliant resin and any color coding you want for commodity or customer identification.

  • Dimensions: internal clearance for product plus any liner or ice.
  • Drainage: hole size and placement tuned to the water load.
  • Ventilation: vent area balanced for cold-chain airflow.
  • Strength: wall gauge and rib design matched to stacked load.

Step 3: Prototype and Validate

Before committing to a production run, validate a prototype against your real conditions. Run boxes through an actual pack-ice-ship-return cycle, confirm they drain and stack as intended, and test them through your wash line. This validation step catches issues — a vent pattern that clogs, a lug that interferes with your palletizer — while changes are still inexpensive. A direct manufacturer can iterate tooling or fabrication quickly during this phase, which is a major advantage over buying a fixed catalog item that almost fits.

Step 4: Confirm Compliance and Documentation

For food-contact use, request documentation confirming the resin and additives meet FDA food-contact requirements. If your boxes handle seafood or will be audited under a food-safety scheme, align the cleaning protocol and material certificate with your HACCP plan. Capturing this paperwork at the order stage means replacement runs match your validated spec and audits go smoothly. For cold-chain applications specifically, review the cold chain and moisture-resistant packaging options that are engineered for iced and refrigerated loads.

Step 5: Place the Production Order and Plan Logistics

Once the prototype is approved, finalize quantities, lead time, and delivery schedule. Because reusable boxes are a capital asset rather than a consumable, size the initial fleet to cover units in transit, in cleaning, and in reserve — typically more than a single cycle's worth. Plan the retrieval and wash loop that keeps the fleet circulating, since reuse economics depend on recovery rates.
