The Complete Guide to Produce Packaging

Polypropylene corrugated produce packaging represent the most significant upgrade available to procurement teams still running single-use cardboard. The material is waterproof, structurally consistent, forklift-compatible, and designed for 50-100 reuse cycles. For industries in agriculture, fresh produce, cold storage, the switch from cardboard to PP corrugated typically pays for itself within one production season.

This guide covers everything buyers need to know about sourcing, specifying, and getting maximum value from produce packaging: material specs, industry applications, import duty considerations, and how to evaluate suppliers in a market full of resellers moving low-quality imports.

Specification Guide for Produce Packaging

Choosing the right produce packaging requires matching wall thickness, flute profile, and resin grade to your specific application. Key specifications:

  • Wall thickness: 4mm for light duty, 6mm for standard, 8mm for heavy duty, 10mm for bulk container applications
  • Flute type: B-flute for top-load stacking strength, C-flute for balanced performance, X-flute (double wall) for maximum rigidity
  • Resin grade: Standard PP for ambient environments, high-density PP for cold chain and freezer applications down to -40°F
  • Food-grade: FDA 21 CFR-compliant virgin PP for direct food contact — required for produce, poultry, seafood, and processed food applications
  • Custom dimensions: Elipacko cuts to custom sizes with lead times varying by volume — contact directly for specifications

Industry Applications

Produce packaging serve a wide range of industries that require durable, reusable, waterproof packaging. Primary applications include agriculture, fresh produce, cold storage. In each of these sectors, the shift from single-use cardboard or wood to reusable PP corrugated delivers measurable improvements in total packaging cost, labor efficiency (no assembly of new boxes each cycle), food safety (hoseable surfaces vs cardboard that harbors bacteria), and sustainability metrics.

Specific to Produce Packaging: buyers in agriculture, fresh produce, cold storage report that properly spec'd produce packaging from quality manufacturers pay back the higher upfront cost within 3-6 months compared to equivalent cardboard volume. The key is sourcing from a manufacturer with consistent resin quality and accurate wall thickness — not from resellers moving underspec product at apparent savings.

Sourcing Produce Packaging in the US Market

The US market for produce packaging is served by a mix of domestic distributors, direct importers, and manufacturer-direct sources. Understanding the supply chain matters for both price and quality:

Manufacturer-direct (recommended): Buying direct from a manufacturer like Elipacko eliminates distributor margin, provides access to custom sizing, and puts you in contact with technical staff who understand load ratings and food-grade requirements. Contact Elipacko directly for factory-direct pricing.

Distributors: Useful for small orders or consolidated buying across multiple product categories. Expect a 30-50% markup over manufacturer pricing. Limited customization capability. Technical support is often limited to what the manufacturer tells them.

Online marketplaces: Convenient for small volumes but impossible to verify quality, resin grade, or accurate wall thickness without samples. High proportion of resellers with no factory relationship and no ability to support custom requirements.
